# 🌊 Question 2 – String Harmonics

## Problem
A guitar string of length 64 cm has a fundamental frequency of 330 Hz. Determine the speed of the wave on the string.

---

## Step 1: Understand the physical situation

The string is fixed at both ends, so it forms standing waves. The fundamental frequency corresponds to the simplest mode of vibration, where the string contains exactly half of a wavelength.

---

## Step 2: Recall the fundamental frequency formula

For a string fixed at both ends, the fundamental frequency is given by:

f = v / (2L)

This comes from the geometry of standing waves, where one half-wavelength fits into the string length.

---

## Step 3: Rearrange the equation to find wave speed

Solving for v:

v = 2Lf

We use this form because we are given the length and frequency.

---

## Step 4: Convert units

The length must be in meters:

L = 64 cm = 0.64 m

Using consistent units is essential to avoid calculation errors.

---

## Step 5: Substitute values and compute

v = 2 × 0.64 × 330 = 422.4 m/s

This gives the speed of the wave traveling along the string.

---

## Step 6: Interpret the result

The wave speed depends on the physical properties of the string (tension and mass density), not on the frequency itself. The frequency changes with mode, but speed remains constant for a given string.

---

## Final Answer

v ≈ 422 m/s
