# 🌊 Question 3 – Superposition Principle

## Problem
Two waves are given by:
y₁(x,t) = A sin(kx − ωt)  
y₂(x,t) = A sin(kx + ωt)  

Find the resulting wave and identify node positions.

---

## Step 1: Understand the physical situation

The two waves have identical amplitude and frequency but travel in opposite directions. This is a classic setup for the formation of a standing wave through superposition.

---

## Step 2: Add the two waves

We form the total displacement by summing:

y = y₁ + y₂

To simplify this expression, we use a trigonometric identity.

---

## Step 3: Apply trigonometric identity

Using:

sin(a − b) + sin(a + b) = 2 sin(a) cos(b)

we obtain:

y = 2A sin(kx) cos(ωt)

---

## Step 4: Interpret the result

This expression separates into a spatial part (sin(kx)) and a time-dependent part (cos(ωt)). This is the defining feature of a standing wave: the shape does not travel, it oscillates in place.

---

## Step 5: Determine node positions

Nodes occur where displacement is always zero:

sin(kx) = 0 → kx = nπ

Since k = 2π/λ, we get:

x = nλ/2

---

## Final Answer

Standing wave:
y = 2A sin(kx) cos(ωt)

Nodes:
x = nλ/2
