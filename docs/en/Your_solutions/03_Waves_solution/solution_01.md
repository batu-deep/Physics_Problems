# 🌊 Question 1 – Wave Properties

## Problem
A sound wave has a frequency of 440 Hz.  
The speed of sound is 343 m/s in air and 1482 m/s in water.  
Find the wavelength in both media.

---

## Step 1: Recall the fundamental wave relation

We start from the basic wave equation:

v = fλ

This equation connects wave speed, frequency, and wavelength. It is the key relationship that describes how waves behave.

At this point, we identify what we know and what we need:
- We know the speed (v)
- We know the frequency (f)
- We want the wavelength (λ)

---

## Step 2: Rearrange the equation

To solve for wavelength, we rearrange the equation:

λ = v / f

We do this because we want to express wavelength directly in terms of known quantities.

---

## Step 3: Understand what changes and what does not

Before calculating, it is very important to understand the physics behind the problem.

The key idea is that **frequency does not change when a wave moves from one medium to another**. This is because the frequency is determined by the source of the wave.

However, the wave speed does change depending on the medium. Since the equation λ = v / f must still hold, any change in speed will directly affect the wavelength.

---

## Step 4: Calculate wavelength in air

Now we substitute the values for air:

λ_air = 343 / 440 ≈ 0.78 m

This means that in air, the distance between repeating points of the wave (such as peaks) is about 0.78 meters.

---

## Step 5: Calculate wavelength in water

Next, we repeat the same calculation for water:

λ_water = 1482 / 440 ≈ 3.37 m

Since the speed of sound is much higher in water, the wavelength becomes significantly larger.

---

## Step 6: Interpret the result

The results clearly show that the wavelength increases when the wave enters a medium where it travels faster.

This can be understood intuitively: if the wave moves faster but still oscillates at the same rate (same frequency), the spacing between oscillations must increase.

---

## Final Answer

λ_air ≈ 0.78 m  
λ_water ≈ 3.37 m

---

## Key Insight

Changing the medium changes the wave speed, and since frequency remains constant, the wavelength adjusts accordingly.
