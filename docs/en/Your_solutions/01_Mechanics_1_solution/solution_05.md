## Question 5 – Relative Velocity

**Problem:**  
A river flows east at 2 m/s. A boat can move at 5 m/s in still water. It wants to go directly north across a 200 m wide river.

---

**Step 1: Understand the situation**  
There are two velocities:
- River velocity (east)
- Boat velocity relative to water

If the boat points directly north, it will drift east.  
So to go straight north, the boat must aim slightly west.

---

**Step 2: Set up velocity components**  
We want the total horizontal velocity to be zero.

So:
Boat’s horizontal component must cancel river velocity.

---

**Step 3: Solve for direction**  
Let θ be the angle west of north.

Horizontal component:
v_x = 5 sinθ

To cancel river:
5 sinθ = 2

→ sinθ = 2/5  
→ θ ≈ 23.6°

---

**Step 4: Find vertical velocity**  
v_y = 5 cosθ

Using Pythagoras:
v_y = √(5² − 2²) = √21 ≈ 4.58 m/s

---

**Step 5: Calculate crossing time**  
t = distance / vertical speed  
t = 200 / 4.58 ≈ 43.7 s

---

**Interpretation:**  
Boat sacrifices some speed sideways to cancel river drift and move straight.

---

**Final Answer:**  
Angle ≈ 23.6° west of north  
Time ≈ 43.7 s
